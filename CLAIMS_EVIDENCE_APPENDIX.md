# Claim and Identification Appendix

This appendix records the evidentiary status of the report's major quantitative claims. It is a guardrail against turning accounting identities, descriptive associations, engineering scenarios, or model-conditional stress tests into causal estimates or forecasts.

## Rating scale

- **A — identified or near-identified:** randomized evidence or a strong quasi-experiment with credible assignment, diagnostics, and robustness.
- **B — robust descriptive/accounting:** transparent estimand and strong measurement, but no exogenous treatment assignment is required or claimed.
- **C — suggestive observational:** a measured association with important selection or omitted-variable threats.
- **D — conditional scenario:** an output conditional on declared engineering, behavioral, policy, or normative assumptions.
- **U — unresolved/unidentified:** current evidence cannot support a direction, magnitude, or probability.

Ratings describe the evidence for the exact claim in the row, not the importance of the topic.

## Bounded ecological corrections and reporting limits

- **Dated scorecard, not eight of nine.** [Analysis 7](analysis/run_analysis_7.py) and [Chart 31](charts/31_planetary_scorecard.png) now reproduce the **Richardson et al. (2023)** nine-process classification (six transgressed), not a current-year reassessment. N/P are one biogeochemical process; aerosols and novel entities are included. LPI is complementary evidence about monitored vertebrate populations, not the biosphere-integrity control variable or a species-extinction percentage.
- **Pressure indicators are not control variables.** [Chart 30](charts/30_nitrogen_water.png) is refreshed from cached fertilizer and water-stress observations without planetary-boundary overlays. The unavailable global-withdrawal panel is explicitly marked missing; no replacement global water total is inferred.
- **Compatible nitrogen accounting.** [Richardson et al. (2023), doi:10.1126/sciadv.adh2458](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adh2458) reports **62 Tg N/yr** for **total industrial plus intentional biological fixation**, with about **190 Tg N/yr** current total. Synthetic-fertilizer inputs cannot be divided by that limit to claim compliance. No new total-N baseline or substitution assumptions have been fabricated. Analysis 18's mixed-flow stack is withdrawn; Analyses 31/34 retain synthetic/input-reduction scenarios only. Analysis 32 no longer publishes the inherited N marginal, joint bounds, copula values, or information rankings. Legacy filenames remain for link continuity; withdrawn CSVs deliberately omit invalid numerical columns.
- **Final-energy chart withdrawn.** [Analysis 17](analysis/run_analysis_17.py) replaces [Chart 90](charts/90_final_energy_tractability.png) with a notice: cement calcination CO2 is not final-energy consumption. Neither deleting a category and renormalizing nor calling the remaining share the “hard 80%” repairs the denominator. Rebuild requires a consistent energy balance and separate process-emissions accounting.
- **LCOS chart withdrawn.** [Chart 86](charts/86_lcos_by_duration.png) no longer shows unsupported manufacturer/pilot LCOS. Capital expenditure in dollars per kWh of capacity is not levelized cost in dollars per MWh discharged. A rebuild needs traceable common assumptions for life, cycles/utilization, efficiency, charging, operation and finance; no target LCOS is inferred from a manufacturer capex claim.
- **Financing comparisons are scale comparisons, not available funding.** IMF explicit plus implicit fossil subsidies include imputed external damages and forgone revenue, not a fully recoverable cash budget. They are not interchangeable with clean-energy investment. ODA is not wholly available for poverty-gap transfers; donor-GNI aid targets are not world-GDP shares. PPP transfer outlays, approximate nominal conversions, aid and fiscal revenue require matching units, years, coverage and incidence. A lower modeled transfer bill excludes the cost of creating the assumed growth and does not establish lower total policy cost.
- **Welfare thresholds remain descriptive and normative.** The GDP/capita cutoffs in [Analysis 18](analysis/run_analysis_18.py) select country-years already above a cutoff; they do not identify an income at which an individual or country causally acquires a “good life.” Current PPP and constant-2015-US-dollar cutoffs are not interchangeable. The new external validation above does not turn these selected criteria into a universal welfare floor.
- **Safe legacy consumers.** [Analysis 26](analysis/run_analysis_26.py) and the dependent probability audit in [Analysis 28](analysis/run_analysis_28.py) now regenerate withdrawal records and notices without reading removed probability columns or fabricating numeric replacements. The archive is explicitly historical. Unchanged energy deployment/mineral estimates remain approximate legacy inputs, not newly source-validated by these chart withdrawals.

## Interpretation rules

1. **Accounting is not causality.** Poverty gaps, decompositions, and stock-flow identities can be exact conditional on their inputs without identifying the policy that produced them.
2. **A failed design is a result.** Failed pre-trends, poor synthetic pre-fit, sparse treated cases, and weak first stages must lower the claim rather than disappear into a footnote.
3. **Stress-range shares are not probabilities.** The global sensitivity outputs answer “how much of this declared parameter space satisfies the condition?” rather than “how likely is the condition?”
4. **Moderation is not institutional causality.** A windfall × governance interaction does not estimate the effect of improving governance.
5. **Natural timing is not automatically natural assignment.** Conflict, WTO entry, investment takeoff, IMF entry, and infrastructure rollout can all be selected responses to expected outcomes.
6. **Ecological impacts remain multidimensional.** Mass, carbon, nitrogen, land, biodiversity, water, and toxicity should not be collapsed into a single damage-weighted tonne without a defensible common welfare function.
